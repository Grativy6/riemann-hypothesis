# One fixed reading under conjugation

Christopher Daniel Pang and Hearthline · 28 November 2026

A swap of the first two coordinates leaves $(1,1,-2)$ fixed. It moves $(2,-1,-1)$. These are the backward and forward readings of the same turn in the example below. Only the backward reading enters the Selmer calculation. A transformation can therefore move much of the coefficient space and still give exactly the conjugation formula the proof needs. [Three-coordinate example](Counterexamples.lean).

## Five callers, one rule

Let $G$ be a group and $x$ a reading satisfying the crossed addition rule
$$
x(gh)=x(g)+A_g x(h).
$$
Each $A_g$ distributes over addition. In this lemma the index $g\mapsto A_g$ is an arbitrary family. A law such as $A_1=I$ was never among its hypotheses.

The maintained proof already contained a lemma saying that if $A_g$ fixes $x(s^{-1})$, then
$$
x(sgs^{-1})= A_s x(g).
$$
The Selmer code had a second version that asked $g$ to fix every vector. We removed that version, simplified the general proof to use additive cancellation, and connected the Selmer span, two mirror identities, the conjugate-dual calculation and Tate-twist vanishing directly to the pointwise lemma. The community change removes thirteen source lines overall. [Original lemma](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/38dc5337b9244e83d252781133df8c4871f7d0f4/src/Lib/ClassField/Places/DecompositionCompletion.lean#L529) · [Community PR #9929](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/pull/9929).

## Where zero is earned

Write $b=x(1)$. The affine map $v\mapsto b+A_1 v$ fixes every reported value by the crossed rule at $1h$. Suppose $A_g x(s^{-1})=x(s^{-1})$. There are now three reported values $u=x(g)$, $v=x(s^{-1})$, and $u+v=x(gs^{-1})$. Apply the map to each. Adding the first two fixed-value equations counts $b$ twice. The equation at $u+v$ counts it once. Additivity and cancellation give $b=0$.

Expand $x(sgs^{-1})$ in the order $s(gs^{-1})$. The sum is $x(s)+A_s x(g)+A_s x(s^{-1})$. Its first and last terms add to $x(1)=0$. This proves the formula with a cancellative commutative additive monoid of values. No ring operation on the scalar labels enters. For a boundary case, the constant integer reading 17 with all $A_g=0$ obeys the raw crossed rule and has $x(1)=17$. The single fixed reading supplies a real condition. [Revised proof](../../src/Lib/ClassField/Places/DecompositionCompletion.lean).

**The fixed reading is also necessary.** For any $u,v,w$ with $x(uv)=x(vw)$, expanding those two products gives
$$
 x(u)=A_v x(w) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A_u x(v)=x(v).
$$
This comparison uses only cancellation and commutative addition. Take $u=sgs^{-1}$, $v=s$, $w=g$. If transport holds, $A_{sgs^{-1}}$ fixes $x(s)$. Apply the forward lemma in this new frame with the inverse turn $s^{-1}$, then use the same comparison once more. It gives $A_g x(s^{-1})=x(s^{-1})$. The fork proves the equivalence in both directions. [Exact pointwise criterion](ReadingTransport.lean).

For the three-coordinate example let $s=(0\,1\,2)$ and $g=(0\,1)$ act on rows by $(A_s v)_i=v_{s^{-1}i}$. Set $v=(2,1,0)$ and $x(h)=v-A_hv$. Then $x(s^{-1})=(1,1,-2)$ and $x(s)=(2,-1,-1)$. The swap fixes the former, and $x(g)=(1,-1,0)$ moves to $x(sgs^{-1})=(0,1,-1)$. Reversing the turn changes which vector must stay fixed. The companion also gives a three-position counterexample after dropping additivity, and a two-position example after replacing cancellative addition with Boolean join. [Exact controls](Counterexamples.lean).

## What the readings determine about the action

For this section the readings lie in an abelian group and the $A_g$ are additive maps on it. Let $U$ be the subgroup generated by all $x(h)$. Let $W$ be generated by $x(h)-b$. The crossed rule gives the following three identities.
$$
 A_g b=0,\qquad A_g x(h)=x(gh)-x(g),\qquad A_1 x(h)=x(h)-b.
$$
Associativity of $(gh)k=g(hk)$ forces $A_g A_h x(k)=A_{gh}x(k)$. Additivity extends this identity to $U$. On $W$, $A_1$ is the identity and $A_{g^{-1}}$ inverts $A_g$. In fact $U=W\oplus\langle b\rangle$ internally. The two summands generate every reading. On their intersection, $A_1$ is both the identity and zero. Thus $A_1$ projects onto the changes along the cyclic offset. Two additive operator families giving the same readings agree on all of $U$. [Decomposition and action on changes](Origin.lean).

The constant correction $\widehat A_g=A_g+I-A_1$ gives a genuine action on $U$. It uses $A_g$ on $W$ and fixes $b$. On an observed point the next reading is $(x(g)-b)+\widehat A_g x(h)$. In the formal three-coordinate control one coordinate moves, another always displays 17, and the last is never reported. On that unread axis the operators explicitly fail to compose. The identities above specify the span where the representation law is earned. [Recentered action](Recenter.lean) · [Ambient counterexample](Counterexamples.lean).

## The three-mark board

Put $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$ and $C=(0,m)$ on a coordinate grid. Fix $A$ and swap the other two labels. To extend the swap multiplied by $k$ to a linear map on the plane, the first column must be $(0,km)$ and the second column $(b,d)$ must satisfy
$$
 mb=k,\qquad md=0.
$$
Over any commutative ring such a map exists exactly when $m$ divides $k$. For an unscaled swap $m$ must be a unit, and the map $(x,y)\mapsto(m^{-1}y,mx)$ then squares to the identity. Over $\mathbb Z/n$ the existence test becomes $\gcd(m,n)\mid k$. When it passes, each entry of the second column has $\gcd(m,n)$ choices, giving exactly $\gcd(m,n)^2$ matrices. This count covers every matrix because the first column is forced. [Linear extension](Triangle.lean) · [Exact count](FiberCount.lean).

| $m$ | $n$ | $k$ | Visible marks | Matrices satisfying the two scaled equations |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 17 | 17 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| 17 | 19 | 1 | 3 | 1 |
| 17 | 289 | 1 | 3 | 0 |
| 17 | 289 | 17 | 3 | 289 |

At 289 the labels remain distinguishable. All six permutations act on the three marks. Only the two fixing $B$ extend to affine maps of the full grid. To see why, reduce further modulo 17. $A$ and $C$ coincide there, so their images under any ambient affine map must still coincide. The two permutations fixing $B$ extend by the identity and $(x,y)\mapsto(x,m-mx-y)$. More generally, if $m$ is a unit all six extend by affine interpolation. If $m$ is a nonunit, reducing modulo the proper ideal $(m)$ forces $B$ to stay fixed. Multiplying by 17 in the last row changes the requested outputs. [Affine interpolation and label symmetries](AffineMarks.lean) · [Offline board](three-mark-board.html).
